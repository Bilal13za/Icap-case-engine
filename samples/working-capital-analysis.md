# Sample 1 — Working-capital pressure (Al-Noor Textiles, fictional)

## Facts
- Revenue is growing strongly (trend: up).
- Cash balance is falling (trend: down).
- Receivables are growing **faster** than sales.
- Supporting figures (Rs 000): receivables 120,000; credit sales 1,460,000;
  inventory 200,000; cost of sales 1,100,000; payables 90,000;
  credit purchases 800,000; current assets 500,000; current liabilities 420,000.

## Issues recognised
**RULE-001 — Working-capital pressure: revenue up, cash down, receivables rising faster than sales**
- Sales growth is not converting into cash. Receivables are absorbing the cash the business needs to operate, which is a classic early sign of working-capital strain even while the profit figure looks healthy.
- *Alternative explanations considered:* Collections have slowed because credit control weakened, not because sales are bad.; Credit terms were deliberately extended to win the extra sales.; A few large credit sales near the period-end distort the receivables figure.; Revenue recognition timing differences rather than a real collection problem.
- *Caution:* Indicator only — not a proven cause. Rule out alternative explanations before concluding.

## Frameworks
- **FW-WCAP** (Working-capital cycle analysis) — Measures the cash conversion cycle (receivable days + inventory days - payable days) to find where cash is trapped.
  - *Not suitable when:* The business has no meaningful working-capital cycle (e.g. pure services, cash sales).
  - *Readiness:* missing_info (missing: sales/purchases)
  - *Why it fits this rule:* Quantifies where cash is trapped via the cash conversion cycle.
- **FW-RATIO** (Ratio analysis) — Liquidity and efficiency ratios (current, quick, turnover days) quantify working-capital pressure.
  - *Not suitable when:* Statements are unavailable or unreliable.
  - *Readiness:* missing_info (missing: financial statements, comparative figures)
  - *Why it fits this rule:* Liquidity and efficiency ratios measure the pressure.
- **FW-TREND** (Trend analysis) — Tracks working-capital ratios over time to distinguish a trend from a one-off movement.
  - *Not suitable when:* Only one period of data exists.
  - *Readiness:* missing_info (missing: multi-period financial data)
  - *Why it fits this rule:* Shows whether the strain is a trend or a one-off.

## Calculations (full workings in the CSV/Excel files)
### Receivable days
- Receivable days = 120,000.00 ÷ 1,460,000.00 × 365.00 = 30.00 days
- **Result: 30.00 days**
- *Customers take on average 30.00 days to pay. Compare with credit terms: a rising figure ties up cash and may signal collection problems.*

### Inventory days
- Inventory days = 200,000.00 ÷ 1,100,000.00 × 365.00 = 66.36 days
- **Result: 66.36 days**
- *Inventory sits on average 66.36 days before sale. A rising figure absorbs cash and may hide obsolete stock.*

### Payable days
- Payable days = 90,000.00 ÷ 800,000.00 × 365.00 = 41.06 days
- **Result: 41.06 days**
- *The business takes on average 41.06 days to pay suppliers. Stretching payables frees cash but risks supplier relationships.*

### Cash conversion cycle
- CCC = 30.00 + 66.36 − 41.06 = 55.30 days
- **Result: 55.30 days**
- *Cash is tied up for 55.30 days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. A lengthening cycle is an early warning of working-capital pressure.*

### Current ratio
- Current ratio = 500,000.00 ÷ 420,000.00 = 1.19:1
- **Result: 1.19 times**
- *Current assets cover current liabilities 1.19 times. Below 1.0 signals potential difficulty meeting short-term obligations.*

## Alternatives considered
Option WC-BORROW: Fund the gap with short-term borrowing -- financial impact: Covers the shortfall at ~14% markup; key risk: Rising finance cost.

## Recommendation
# Recommendation: Option WC-TIGHTEN -- Tighten working-capital management

## Executive summary
Option WC-TIGHTEN is preferable because Case facts and calculations below. The financial analysis indicates Cash conversion cycle is 55.30 days and lengthening. However, the decision depends on Sales growth continues at the current pace. Management should Set formal credit limits and collection targets and monitor Receivable days.

## Supporting facts
- Revenue is growing (trend: up)
- Cash balance is falling (trend: down)
- Receivables are growing faster than sales
- Receivable days = 30.00; inventory days = 66.36; payable days = 41.06

## Financial analysis
- Cash conversion cycle is 55.30 days and lengthening
- Current ratio is 1.19 times — thin cover

## Key assumption
Sales growth continues at the current pace

## Risks of the recommended option
- Customer pushback on stricter terms

## Alternatives considered
- Option WC-BORROW: Fund the gap with short-term borrowing -- financial impact: Covers the shortfall at ~14% markup; key risk: Rising finance cost.

## Conditions that would change this recommendation
- Receivable days rising above 45
- Bank withdrawing the overdraft facility

## Implementation steps
1. Set formal credit limits and collection targets
2. Review slow-moving inventory lines monthly

## KPIs to monitor
- Receivable days
- Cash conversion cycle
- Overdraft utilisation

## Review timing
Monthly for the next two quarters
